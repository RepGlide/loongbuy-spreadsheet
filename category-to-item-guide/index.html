Category-to-Item Guide

Which context survives moving from a category entry to a specific link, and which facts must be re-read on the current page.

Moving down a level

When you step from a category entry into a specific link, you move from a wider level to a narrower one. Some context carries over; some must be re-read from scratch.

What you can carry over

  • The direction. Which category you came from and what kind of thing you are looking for.
  • The entry point. The link you followed and the general range it pointed to.
  • The broad category context. The type of product, as long as it stays at the category level.

What you must re-read

  • The specific option. Color, size, version, and quantity — these live on the current page, not in the category label.
  • The current price. The category cannot tell you what the page shows now.
  • The stock state. Availability is a current-page fact, not a category fact.
  • The images. A category thumbnail is a clue, not the item’s actual images or options.
A category label narrows the range. It does not prove any specific option, price, or stock state — those must be re-read on the current page.

Worked reading

  1. From the category: keep the direction and the broad type.
  2. On the listing: note what is visibly shown, without assuming it is complete.
  3. On the current page: re-read option, price, stock, and images there.
  4. At the boundary: if the current page does not show a detail, do not carry it over from the category.

Resolution example — not product detail, image analysis, database depth, or verification.
