Browse Depth Guide

The four resolution levels — overview, category, listing, and current page — and the question boundary each one sets.

One conclusion, one level

Every conclusion comes from a specific level of browse depth. A conclusion is only as specific as the level it came from, so the first step is always to name the level before you trust the claim.

The four levels

  • Overview — citron.

    Direction and entry point only. It shows where to start and what is broadly available. It points, it does not prove.

  • Category — lavender.

    Narrows the product range without confirming any specific option, price, or stock state. A smaller frame, still not a proof.

  • Listing — crimson.

    The visible clues at hand. It shows what is currently in front of you, which may still lack full context.

  • Current page — cobalt.

    The specific option and its details, read again on the page you are actually viewing right now.

The question boundary

Each level answers a different question, and a wider level cannot answer the narrower one’s question.

  • Overview answers where do I start?
  • Category answers what kind of thing is this?
  • Listing answers what is shown right now?
  • Current page answers what does the page say now?
A wider label is not a substitute for the current page’s specific options and facts. When the evidence runs out, stop — do not zoom past it.

A worked reading

  1. Overview: the resource points you toward a group of categories.
  2. Category: you pick one category, which narrows the range.
  3. Listing: you see the clues currently displayed for that range.
  4. Current page: you open the specific option and re-read its details there.

Resolution example — not product detail, image analysis, database depth, or verification.
